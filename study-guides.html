📖 Study Guides

Short, practical guides to help you understand a topic before testing your recall with MCQs.

English Grammar: A Practical Study Method

Grammar is the system that explains how words work together in sentences. A useful way to study grammar is to move from identification to application: first identify the form, then understand its function, and finally use it in your own sentence.

1. Identify the word or structure

Ask what role the target word plays. For example, in “The child runs quickly,” child is a noun, runs is a verb, and quickly is an adverb.

Example: “She speaks softly.” The word softly tells us how she speaks, so it is an adverb of manner.

2. Learn the rule with context

Do not memorise a rule without examples. Compare similar sentences and notice what changes when the grammatical form changes.

3. Test yourself

After studying a rule, answer questions without looking at the explanation. Review every mistake and write a new example sentence.

English Vocabulary: Learn Words in Context

Vocabulary knowledge is more than knowing a translation. A strong learner can recognise a word, understand it in context, and use it naturally.

Four useful steps

  • Learn the central meaning and part of speech.
  • Read at least one natural example sentence.
  • Notice common word combinations and nearby context.
  • Recall the word later without looking at the answer.
Example: perilous means dangerous or risky. “The climbers began a perilous journey.” The sentence shows how the adjective is used with a noun.

When two words look similar, compare their meanings and usage rather than memorising them separately. This reduces confusion and improves practical vocabulary.

Bangladesh Studies: Organise Facts by Topic

Bangladesh studies includes history, geography, government, culture, economics, Liberation War history, and many other areas. Long lists of disconnected facts are difficult to retain, so organise them into groups.

  • History: connect important events with their period, causes, people, and consequences.
  • Geography: connect locations with rivers, borders, regions, physical features, and neighbouring countries.
  • Government and administration: distinguish stable historical information from details that may change over time.
  • Culture: connect literature, traditions, festivals, landmarks, and notable personalities with their context.

For important examination preparation, compare quiz information with authoritative textbooks and official publications, especially when a fact can change.

General Knowledge: Build Connections

General knowledge grows when facts are connected. If you learn a country, for example, connect its continent, capital, neighbouring countries, major physical features, history, and important institutions.

Active recall

Close your notes and try to answer a question from memory. This is more demanding than rereading, but it gives you useful information about what you actually remember.

Review mistakes

Do not simply move past an incorrect answer. Ask why it was wrong, learn the correct fact, and return to the question after a gap.

Simple review cycle: Learn → Recall → Check → Explain → Repeat.

A simple seven-day revision plan

This flexible plan can be used for vocabulary, grammar, Bangladesh studies, or general knowledge. Adjust the amount of work to your available time; consistency and careful review matter more than rushing through many questions.

  • Day 1 — Learn: choose one small topic. Read the rule or explanation and note the main ideas.
  • Day 2 — Recall: attempt a short quiz without notes. Mark uncertain answers as well as incorrect ones.
  • Day 3 — Correct: review each mistake and write a short reason why the right answer is correct.
  • Day 4 — Apply: make your own example sentence, compare two similar concepts, or connect related facts.
  • Day 5 — Re-test: answer the topic questions again without looking at the previous result.
  • Day 6 — Mix: combine this topic with one previously studied topic to practise choosing the right knowledge.
  • Day 7 — Review: list what you now know, what still causes confusion, and what needs another review next week.
Example review note: “I confused affect and effect. I will write one sentence with each word and test myself again tomorrow.” A specific note gives you a clearer next step than simply writing “wrong answer.”

A four-step method for reviewing MCQs

Use the same review method across subjects so that every quiz becomes a learning activity rather than a score-only exercise.

  1. Answer first: choose an option before revealing the explanation, even if you are unsure.
  2. Check the reasoning: read the explanation and identify the clue, rule, or fact that supports the answer.
  3. Compare alternatives: understand why the most tempting incorrect option does not fit.
  4. Retrieve later: return to the question after a delay and try to explain the answer without help.

For factual subjects, verify uncertain or time-sensitive details with trustworthy reference material. For language learning, go beyond the definition and check how the word or grammar structure works in a complete sentence.

Keep a personal mistake log

A score tells you how many answers were correct; a mistake log helps you decide what to study next. After each practice session, record only the questions that were incorrect or uncertain.

  • Topic: write the subject and the specific rule, word, event, or fact.
  • My choice: note why the option looked convincing.
  • Correct reasoning: explain the clue or evidence that supports the right answer.
  • Next review: revisit the question the next day, several days later, and again the following week.
Sample entry:
Topic: English vocabulary — perilous
Problem: I confused it with “permanent.”
Correction: Perilous means dangerous or risky; permanent means lasting or not temporary.
Practice: Write one original sentence for each word, then test the distinction again later.

Do not copy an explanation without understanding it. Rewrite the idea in your own words. If a factual answer seems inconsistent with an authoritative textbook or official source, record the question and report it for review rather than memorising a doubtful claim.

How quiz quality is reviewed

A useful practice question should have clear wording, one defensible best answer, relevant options, and an explanation that teaches the underlying rule or fact. Technical checks—such as counting questions and checking answer indexes—help catch code problems, but they do not replace factual review.
