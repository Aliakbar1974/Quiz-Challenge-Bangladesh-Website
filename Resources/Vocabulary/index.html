LEARN WORDS IN CONTEXT

English Vocabulary Learning Resources

Build a strong vocabulary by understanding what a word means, how it behaves in a sentence, which words commonly appear with it, and when it is appropriate to use it. Learn first; practise with MCQs afterwards.

How to study a new word

  1. Pronunciation: listen to or read the pronunciation and notice the stressed syllable.
  2. Part of speech: identify whether the word is a noun, verb, adjective or adverb. The same spelling can sometimes have more than one grammatical role.
  3. Meaning: learn the central English meaning and a suitable Bengali equivalent. A translation is a guide, not a complete substitute for context.
  4. Word relationships: compare synonyms, antonyms and related forms. Check whether the alternatives can really replace the word in the same sentence.
  5. Usage: study a natural example and common word combinations, then write a new sentence of your own.
  6. Recall: cover the meaning and retrieve it from memory. Review difficult words after a gap rather than rereading them immediately.

Important: Do not memorise a long list of Bengali translations alone. English words can have more than one sense, and synonyms are rarely interchangeable in every context.

Who can use these lessons?

The material is designed to support school learners, Cadet College admission candidates, university admission candidates, job seekers and BCS candidates. Learners should begin with the example sentences and gradually move towards shades of meaning, formal usage and more demanding vocabulary.

Dictionary references

For pronunciation, additional senses, usage labels and independent checking, consult reputable learner and general dictionaries:

  • Cambridge Dictionary — learner-friendly definitions, pronunciation and examples.
  • Oxford Learner’s Dictionaries — usage information, grammar patterns and pronunciation.
  • Merriam-Webster — definitions, word history and related forms.
