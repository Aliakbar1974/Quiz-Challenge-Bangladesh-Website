Transformation of Sentences

Practise changing sentence structure while preserving meaning, including simple, compound, complex, affirmative and negative forms.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain the rules, recognise the target forms in context, avoid common errors and apply the patterns to new sentences.

  • Understand the core terms and distinctions.
  • Study examples in complete sentences, not as isolated rules.
  • Complete all 15 practice items before opening the answer key.

1. Preserve meaning first

Sentence transformation changes grammatical structure while keeping the original meaning as closely as possible. Check tense, emphasis, logic and reference after rewriting.

Although he was poor, he was honest. → In spite of being poor, he was honest.

2. Simple, compound and complex

A simple sentence has one independent clause; a compound sentence joins independent clauses; a complex sentence includes a dependent clause. Transformations often replace a clause with a phrase or join/split clauses.

Because he was ill, he stayed home. → He was ill, so he stayed home.

3. Contrast and concession

Although/even though can often be transformed into despite/in spite of + noun or gerund. Keep the logical subject and tense clear.

Although she was tired, she continued. → Despite being tired, she continued.

4. Degree and comparison

Comparative and superlative forms can be transformed with patterns such as no other, one of the, as…as and not so…as. Avoid changing the scope of the claim.

Rina is the tallest girl in the class. → No other girl in the class is as tall as Rina.

5. Affirmative, negative and exclamatory patterns

Some affirmative and negative forms are equivalent in context, but not every sentence has an exact equivalent. Transform only when the meaning remains intact; do not mechanically add not.

Everyone admired her. → There was no one who did not admire her.

Guided practice

Try each item first. Then open the answer key and read the explanation for any answer you missed.

  1. Transform with “in spite of”: Although he was ill, he attended class.
  2. Change to compound: “Because it was raining, we stayed home.”
  3. Change to complex: “He worked hard and succeeded.”
  4. Transform: “No other metal is as useful as iron” into superlative form.
  5. Transform using “too…to”: “The box is so heavy that I cannot lift it.”
  6. Transform using “enough”: “He is so strong that he can carry it.”
  7. Change to negative without changing meaning: “Only Mina solved the puzzle.”
  8. Transform: “As soon as the bell rang, the students left” using “No sooner.”
  9. Change to comparative: “Rafi is the tallest boy in the group.”
  10. Transform with “despite”: “Although she had little time, she helped us.”
  11. Change to exclamatory: “It is a very beautiful scene.”
  12. Transform: “He is too weak to walk” using “so…that.”
  13. Change to simple sentence: “When he saw the police, he ran away.”
  14. Transform: “Very few cities are as large as Dhaka” into comparative form.
  15. What must remain unchanged in a sentence transformation?
Show answer key and explanations
  1. In spite of being ill, he attended class. — In spite of is followed by a noun or gerund phrase.
  2. It was raining, so we stayed home. — The dependent reason clause becomes an independent clause linked by so.
  3. He succeeded because he worked hard. — A dependent reason clause creates a complex sentence.
  4. Iron is the most useful metal. — The equivalent superlative expresses the same ranking claim.
  5. The box is too heavy for me to lift. — Too + adjective + for + object + infinitive expresses inability.
  6. He is strong enough to carry it. — Adjective + enough + infinitive expresses sufficient ability.
  7. None but Mina solved the puzzle. — None but is a formal equivalent of only in this pattern.
  8. No sooner had the bell rung than the students left. — No sooner is followed by inversion and paired with than.
  9. Rafi is taller than any other boy in the group. — Any other avoids comparing Rafi with himself.
  10. Despite having little time, she helped us. — Despite is followed by a noun phrase or gerund phrase.
  11. What a beautiful scene it is! — What + a/an + adjective + singular count noun forms an exclamation.
  12. He is so weak that he cannot walk. — So + adjective + that-clause conveys the result.
  13. Seeing the police, he ran away. — A participial phrase can replace the time clause when the subject is the same.
  14. Dhaka is larger than most other cities. — Very few + as…as expresses a high ranking, but not necessarily an absolute superlative.
  15. The intended meaning and logical relationship. — A structural change is correct only when it preserves the meaning.

Revision checklist

  • I can explain the main rules in my own words.
  • I can recognise the correct form in a complete sentence.
  • I can explain why a common incorrect form is wrong.
  • I can apply the rule to a new example.

If an answer was wrong, identify the rule behind the mistake and try writing two original examples.
