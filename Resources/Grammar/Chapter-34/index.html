Advanced Articles and Determiners

Use a, an, the and zero article accurately with abstract and uncountable nouns, institutions, geography and generalisations.

Estimated study time: 12–18 minutes · 5 lesson sections · 15 practice questions with explanations

Your learning path

1. LearnRead the rules and examples carefully.
2. PractiseAnswer all 15 questions before revealing the key.
3. ReviewExplain each mistake and write a new example.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain the rules, recognise the target forms in context, avoid common errors and apply the patterns to new sentences.

  • Understand the core terms and distinctions.
  • Use examples to connect grammar rules with real sentences.
  • Complete all 15 practice items before opening the answer key.

1. Countability comes first

Singular countable nouns normally need a determiner. Uncountable nouns such as advice, information and furniture usually do not take a/an or a plural -s in their ordinary meanings.

She gave me some advice, not an advice.

2. A/an and the

Use a/an for a non-specific singular count noun or first mention; use the for something identifiable to the reader or already mentioned. The choice depends on sound, not spelling.

I saw an hourglass and a university.

3. Zero article and general meaning

Plural count nouns and uncountable nouns often take no article when speaking generally. Use the when referring to a specific group or item.

Books can broaden the mind. / The books on this shelf are mine.

4. Institutions and geographical names

Some institutional expressions use zero article when referring to their usual purpose (go to school, be in prison), while the may identify a particular building or specific institution. Country and place names follow conventions, with exceptions such as the United States and the Netherlands.

She goes to school. / Her father went to the school to meet the teacher.

5. Determiners and fixed patterns

Some, any, much, many, few, a few, little and a little express different quantities or attitudes. A few/a little mean some; few/little emphasise scarcity.

We have a little time left, so we can review one more example.

Guided practice

Try each item first without checking the answers. Afterward, open the key and read the explanation for every uncertain answer.

  1. She gave me ___ useful advice. (a / some)
  2. He is ___ honest man. (a / an)
  3. She studies at ___ university in Dhaka. (a / an)
  4. ___ sun rises in the east. (The / A)
  5. ___ Tigers are endangered animals. (The / —)
  6. I usually have ___ breakfast at seven. (— / the)
  7. We visited ___ United States last year. (the / —)
  8. There are ___ students in the room, but not many. (a few / few)
  9. We have ___ water left, enough for tea. (a little / little)
  10. Could you give me ___ information about the course? (an / some)
  11. She went to ___ school to speak to the headteacher. (school / the school)
  12. I need ___ umbrella because it is raining. (a / an)
  13. Do you have ___ questions? (some / any)
  14. ___ rich should help the vulnerable. (The / A)
  15. Choose the correct phrase: “many furniture” or “much furniture.”
Show answer key and explanations
  1. some — Advice is uncountable in its ordinary meaning, so some advice is standard.
  2. an — Honest begins with a vowel sound because h is silent.
  3. a — University begins with the consonant sound /j/ (“you”), so use a.
  4. The — The sun is a unique, identifiable object in this context.
  5. — — Zero article is used for plural nouns in a general statement.
  6. — — Meals generally take no article when mentioned in their ordinary, general sense.
  7. the — The United States conventionally takes the.
  8. a few — A few means some; few emphasises that there are almost none.
  9. a little — A little means some amount, enough for the stated purpose.
  10. some — Information is uncountable and takes some, not an.
  11. the school — The sentence means she visited the particular building, not attended school as a pupil.
  12. an — Umbrella begins with a vowel sound.
  13. any — Any is common in neutral questions asking whether something exists.
  14. The — The + adjective can refer to a group of people as a class.
  15. much furniture — Furniture is uncountable; much furniture is grammatical, though a lot of furniture is often more natural.

  • I can explain why a common incorrect form is wrong.
