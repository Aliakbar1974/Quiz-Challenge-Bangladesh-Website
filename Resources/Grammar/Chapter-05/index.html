GRAMMAR RESOURCE · CHAPTER 05

Articles and Determiners

Learn a, an, the and zero article, plus demonstratives, quantifiers and other determiners with pronunciation-based rules, examples and exam practice.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain the main rules, identify the target forms in real sentences, avoid common errors and apply the rules to unfamiliar examples.

  • Understand the core terms and distinctions.
  • Study examples in context rather than memorising isolated rules.
  • Complete the practice before opening the answer key.

1. What do articles do?

Articles help show whether a noun is being introduced generally or identified specifically. A and an are indefinite articles; the is the definite article. Sometimes no article is needed, a pattern called the zero article.

FormCommon useExample
a / anOne non-specific countable singular nouna book, an orange
theIdentifiable, previously mentioned or unique in contextthe book on the desk
zero articleMany plural/uncountable nouns used generally; many proper namesBooks are useful. Water is essential.

2. A or an: listen to the first sound

Choose a or an according to the first sound of the following word, not simply its first written letter.

a university (starts with /y/ sound), a European country, a one-way street (/w/ sound).
an hour (silent h), an honest person, an MBA student (letter name begins with a vowel sound).

Use a/an with a singular countable noun when it is not identified as a particular one: “I saw a bird.” When the listener knows which bird, use “The bird was blue.”

3. When to use “the”

  • When the noun has already been introduced: “I saw a dog. The dog was wet.”
  • When a phrase or context identifies it: “Please close the door.”
  • With something treated as unique in the context: “the sun,” “the principal” (at a particular school).
  • With superlatives and ordinal numbers in many ordinary uses: “the tallest building,” “the first chapter.”
  • With many rivers, seas, oceans and plural mountain ranges: “the Padma,” “the Bay of Bengal,” “the Himalayas.”

Do not assume every geographical name takes the. Most country and city names do not (“Bangladesh,” “Dhaka”), while some country names do (“the United States,” “the United Kingdom”).

4. Zero article and general meaning

General plural: Students need practice.
General uncountable: Education can change lives.
Specific: The students in this class need practice. The education she received was excellent.

Institutions, meals, languages, academic subjects and transport expressions have patterns with exceptions: “go to school” (as a student), “have breakfast,” “speak English,” “study mathematics,” “travel by bus.” Compare “go to the school” when referring to a particular building or visit.

5. Determiners beyond articles

DeterminerTypical useExample
this / thatSingular or uncountable referencethis idea, that water
these / thosePlural referencethese books, those houses
some / anyQuantity; any often in questions/negativessome rice; do you have any questions?
much / manyMuch with uncountable; many with countable pluralmuch time; many days
few / a fewCountable plural; few suggests hardly any, a few means somea few friends
little / a littleUncountable; little suggests hardly any, a little means somea little water
each / everyIndividual members; usually singular nouneach student; every day
“Few people understood” suggests almost none. “A few people understood” means some people did. “Little time remained” suggests not enough; “a little time remained” means some time was available.

Guided practice

Try each item first. Then open the answer key and read the explanation for any answer you missed.

  1. Choose: ___ honest man. (a / an)
  2. Choose: ___ university in the city. (a / an)
  3. Complete: I bought a pen. ___ pen is blue.
  4. Choose for general meaning: ___ Tigers are mammals. (The / —)
  5. Complete: We sailed across ___ Bay of Bengal.
  6. Choose: She speaks ___ English. (the / —)
  7. Choose: How ___ books did you buy? (much / many)
  8. Choose: How ___ milk is left? (much / many)
  9. Which means “some friends”: few friends / a few friends?
  10. Complete: There isn't ___ sugar left. (many / much)
  11. Choose: ___ books on this table are mine. (These / This)
  12. Which is natural for a student attending lessons: go to school / go to the school?
  13. Choose: He is ___ tallest player on the team.
  14. Complete: I need ___ information about the course. (an / some)
  15. What is the difference between “little time” and “a little time”?
Show answer key and explanations
  1. an — Honest begins with a vowel sound because h is silent.
  2. a — University begins with the consonant /y/ sound.
  3. The — The second mention identifies the pen already introduced.
  4. — (no article) — Plural countable nouns often take zero article for general statements.
  5. the — Names of bays commonly take the.
  6. — (no article) — Languages usually take zero article.
  7. many — Books is a countable plural noun.
  8. much — Milk is uncountable in this meaning.
  9. a few friends — A few means some; few stresses that the number is very small.
  10. much — Sugar is uncountable.
  11. These — These is used with plural nouns.
  12. go to school — Zero article commonly refers to the institution's usual purpose; the school means a particular building.
  13. the — Superlatives commonly take the.
  14. some — Information is normally uncountable and does not take an.
  15. Little = hardly any; a little = some. — The article changes the usual quantity meaning.

Revision checklist

  • I can explain the main rule in my own words.
  • I can identify the target form in a sentence.
  • I can explain why a common incorrect example is wrong.
  • I can apply the rule in a new sentence.

Do not judge mastery only by a score. If an answer was wrong, identify the rule behind the mistake and practise that pattern again.
